What is PM2.5?

PM2.5 refers to fine inhalable particles with a diameter of 2.5 micrometers or smaller. These particles are about 30 times smaller than a human hair and can travel deep into the lungs.

Because of their tiny size, PM2.5 can enter the bloodstream and affect the lungs, heart, and brain. The World Health Organization identifies PM2.5 as one of the most harmful air pollutants.

Did You Know?

  • PM2.5 is one of the deadliest forms of air pollution.
  • It can travel deep into the lungs and bloodstream.
  • Long-term exposure increases risk of heart and lung diseases.
  • Children breathe faster and inhale more PM2.5 per body weight.

How Small is PM2.5?

  • Human hair: ~70 micrometers
  • Sand grain: ~90 micrometers
  • PM10: 10 micrometers
  • PM2.5: 2.5 micrometers

Health Impacts of PM2.5

  • Breathing & Lungs: Breathing difficulty, asthma attacks, coughing, wheezing, and reduced lung function. Long-term exposure can slow lung growth in children.
  • Heart & Blood Vessels: Increased risk of heart disease, high blood pressure, heart attacks, and stroke.
  • Brain & Nervous System: Headaches, fatigue, reduced concentration, and long-term links to dementia.
  • Pregnancy: Higher risk of low birth weight, preterm birth, and pregnancy complications.
  • Life Expectancy: Long-term exposure can reduce overall lifespan.
  • Children & Elderly: More hospital visits, more days of illness, and greater vulnerability.

What Everyone Can Do

  • Walk, cycle, or use public transport; share rides.
  • Avoid burning waste; use clean cooking options.
  • Save energy; support rooftop solar.
  • Plant and protect trees.
  • Check AQI before outdoor activities.
  • Wear N95 masks on high pollution days.

CO₂, Fossil Fuels & Climate Change

The same activities that cause local air pollution also release CO₂, driving climate change.

  • More CO₂ → Heatwaves, floods, sea-level rise.
  • Hot days trap pollutants near the ground.
  • Cutting fossil fuels improves health now and climate later.
