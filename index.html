What is PM2.5?

PM2.5 refers to fine inhalable particles with a diameter of 2.5 micrometers or smaller. These particles are about 30 times smaller than a human hair and can travel deep into the lungs.

Because of their tiny size, PM2.5 can enter the bloodstream and affect the lungs, heart, and brain. The World Health Organization identifies PM2.5 as one of the most harmful air pollutants.

How Small is PM2.5?

  • Human hair: ~70 µm
  • Sand grain: ~90 µm
  • PM10: 10 µm
  • PM2.5: 2.5 µm

PM2.5 is so small that it can bypass the nose and throat and reach deep into the lungs.

WHO limits are much stricter than Indian standards. Many Indian cities, including Kochi, often exceed WHO guidelines.

PM2.5 and “Cigarette Equivalent”

To make air pollution more relatable, some studies compare daily PM2.5 exposure to “cigarettes per day”. Exact numbers vary, but a rough idea:

  • PM2.5 around 22–25 µg/m³ (24h) ≈ breathing the smoke of about 1 cigarette per day.
  • Higher PM2.5 for many days can add up to the equivalent of smoking many cigarettes over a year.

This does not mean you are literally smoking, but it shows how serious long-term exposure can be. Reducing PM2.5 protects lungs and heart just like avoiding cigarettes.

Health Impacts of PM2.5

  • Lungs: Asthma attacks, chronic cough, reduced lung growth in children.
  • Heart: Higher risk of heart attack, stroke, high blood pressure.
  • Brain: Headache, fatigue, reduced concentration; long-term links to dementia.
  • Pregnancy: Low birth weight, preterm birth, complications.
  • Children & Elderly: More hospital visits, more days of illness.

Pollution Hotspots – Kochi Region

Traffic corridors, industrial areas, and construction zones often show higher PM2.5 and PM10 levels.

  • Kalamassery & Eloor – industrial belt
  • Edappally & Vyttila – traffic junctions
  • MG Road – commercial corridor
  • Container Road – heavy truck movement
  • Kakkanad – IT hub, traffic congestion, construction dust

Smart Solutions for Cleaner Kochi

Transport & Traffic

  • Phase out old, high-emission vehicles.
  • Create clean air zones around schools and hospitals.
  • Promote electric buses and shared mobility.
  • Discourage idling (>60 seconds) at junctions.
  • Stricter and regular PUC checks.

Urban Planning & Industry

  • Better road maintenance to reduce dust.
  • Emission controls in industrial belts.
  • More AQI monitoring stations across Kochi.
  • Green buffers near busy roads.

What Can Individuals Do?

  • Use public transport or carpool instead of solo driving.
  • Avoid burning waste, leaves, or plastic.
  • Maintain vehicles regularly to reduce emissions.
  • Install HEPA filters in home air purifiers and HVAC systems.
  • Use HEPA cabin filters in cars to reduce exposure while commuting.
  • Check AQI before outdoor activities and plan your day accordingly.
  • Wear N95 masks on high pollution days.
  • Plant and care for native trees in your neighbourhood.
  • Encourage schools and offices to monitor indoor air quality.

Clean Air Starts Indoors

  • Use HEPA-based air purifiers in bedrooms and living rooms.
  • Ensure good ventilation when outdoor AQI is moderate or better.
  • Keep windows closed on very high pollution days.
  • Avoid incense, indoor smoking, and frequent use of strong chemical sprays.
  • Regularly clean filters in AC and purifiers.

What is an Algae Bioreactor?

An algae bioreactor is a closed, transparent chamber where microalgae grow using light, water, and carbon dioxide (CO₂). These tiny organisms naturally absorb CO₂ from the air and release oxygen through photosynthesis.

  • Closed & safe: No algae escape; no smell; no contamination.
  • CO₂ capture: Algae absorb CO₂ from indoor or outdoor air.
  • O₂ release: Clean oxygen is returned to the environment.
  • Low energy: Works mainly on sunlight or LED light.
  • Nature-based: Uses the same mechanism as trees – photosynthesis.

Algae bioreactors are used in research labs and green buildings to demonstrate how nature-based solutions can support cleaner air and climate action.
